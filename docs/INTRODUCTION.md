# Introduction
Hello and welcome to Compos, the composable computer for knowledge work.

Back in the day when the early programming paradigms were invented, the computer was the preserve of university mathematics professors. And these guys didn't mess around. When they weren't squeezing every last ounce of performance out of their algorithms, they were designing interfaces that let them fly through the information space like a frog on a rocket. 

With the advent of a more popular Internet many of these interfaces got dumbed down to the point that 9/10 computer users are now wandering their info landscapes with a profound learned helplessness. 

It is seen as normal to leave the environment you are working in to get information from elsewhere. The old dudes wouldn't be having any of it. Emacs remains the only editor I have seen which as a `scroll-other-window' method that you use for skimming documentation in the second pane. It is such a small touch and so evocative of the kind of programmer that needed such a feature.

# Context has become king

For many years, it was even possible to live like a normie. Switching tabs to check mail. Switching desktops then switching browsers then switching tabs to check Sentry errors. I mean can you imagine?

But that was before the context exploded and you had machine intelligence by your side. Well, not by your side really. Stuck in a terminal window. Stuck in a browser sidebar. Stuck in one more walled garden app that is only designed to suck data through an mcp straw.


## There is another way
I know this because I have lived hankering for something like emacs. There's this [video by Tsoding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DMbrNhx2zWQ) where he says never use emacs. Because it is the most annoyingly useful piece of software and of course too clunky to use anymore. The lack of a graphical interface is fatal for widespread consumer use. But the UX ideas! Pure gold. Just imagine that ux in a graphical environment, with machine intelligence one shortcut key away.

## A home for malleable apps.

The intelligence can write you any app you want. It can write apps that pull context from where it is to where you can do something with it. Bring in your fireflies transcripts. Bring in your sentry reports. Bring in your email. Your news reader. Even the sites you browse. Bring them into a place where the context isn't theoretical at the end of a chain of hopefully correct mcp tool calls. It's there - in that buffer. you can point chat at that buffer and it will know what you're talking about.
