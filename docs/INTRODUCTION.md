# Introduction
Hello and welcome to Compos, the composable computer for knowledge work.

Back in the day when the early programming paradigms were invented, the computer was the preserve of university mathematics professors. And these guys didn't mess around. When they weren't squeezing every last ounce of performance out of their algorithms, they were designing interfaces that let them fly through the information space like a frog on a rocket. 

With the advent of a more popular Internet many of these interfaces got dumbed down to the point that 9/10 computer users are now wandering their info landscapes with a profound learned helplessness. 

It is seen as normal to leave the environment you are working in to get information from elsewhere. The old dudes wouldn't be having any of it. Emacs remains the only editor I have seen which as a `scroll-other-window` method that you use for skimming documentation in the second pane. It is such a small touch and so evocative of the kind of programmer that needed such a feature.


It is time to now go back to powerful interfaces. Back in the day, learning the mantras and incantations of the esoteric Church of Emacs was an intellectual feat by itself. Emacs became the forever project, the endless customisation, almost a magic cloak fitting snug on your body.

With LLMs that power is now available to everyone. I suggest we dive in.

## Context has become king

What is the neckbeard hitting `scroll-other-window` saying with this action? He's saying that he doesn't go anywhere. What he wants he summons to him. 

For many years,  when the Internet was simple, it was possible to go to where the thing was. No longer. We all have multiple chrome profiles and multiple browsers in multiple desktops. Writing a personal email and want to see server health for that third client? swipe desktop swipe desktop, switch to browser, search for tab, wrong window, cmd tab cmd tab....I mean can you imagine?

You have all powerful machine intelligence and it can't see which tab you're in. You're installing extension after extension to do this. the tui agents live in their own world. The GUI agents, well they stick their archived chats behind the settings page.

GUIs are not great for power surfing anyways. Someone else's interface can never be.

## There is another way
I know this because I have lived hankering for something like emacs. There's this [video by Tsoding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DMbrNhx2zWQ) where he says never use emacs. ## A home for malleable apps.

Emacs has always been the home for malleable apps. Heavyweights like magit and org-mode probably started as little snippets of elisp written to solve smaller problems. But with malleable software, you can grow it as much as you want.

The intelligence can write you any app you want. It can write apps that pull context from where it is to where you can do something with it. Bring in your fireflies transcripts. Bring in your sentry reports. Bring in your email. Your news reader. Even the sites you browse. Bring them into a place where the context isn't theoretical at the end of a chain of hopefully correct mcp tool calls. It's there - in that buffer. you can point chat at that buffer and it will know what you're talking about.

need a sentry app? just ask for one.

![image](/Users/svs/src/ai-max.el/docs/sentry.png)

Imagine. Pulling all of your context to you instead of going there. What a luxury. You can ask the agent to build you a button that asks an agent to solve the issue. Or resolve it from right there.

Compos'consistent interface means that your same keybindings work everywhere. Marking items in a list. Taking action on marked items. Filtering lists. All work exactly the same no matter where the data comes from. Pop an API key in your secrets.scm and bid the agent fetcheth your data to you.
